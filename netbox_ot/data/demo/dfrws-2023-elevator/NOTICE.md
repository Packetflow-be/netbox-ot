# Capture demo data: DFRWS 2023 "The Troubled Elevator"

The files in this directory (`assets.json`, `flows.json`, `summary.json`) are derived from the network trace of
the **DFRWS 2023 Forensic Challenge "The Troubled Elevator"**, created by the Security and Forensics Engineering
(SAFE) Lab at Virginia Commonwealth University for DFRWS:

- Source: https://github.com/dfrws/dfrws2023-challenge (`Network Trace/`)
- Licence: Apache License 2.0, https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0
- Challenge organisers: Irfan Ahmed (DFRWS Challenge Chair), Wooyeon Jo, Adeen Ayub, Muhammad Haris Rais, Hala
  Ali, Nehal Ameen, Muhammad Ahsan, Syed Ali Qasim

## What was changed

The trace (`142728_162728.pcapng`, as mirrored unmodified in the `data-v1` release of
SackOfHacks/ICS-PCAP-Challenges, SHA-256 of the archive in `.github/bench/datasets.json`) was run through
Malcolm's Zeek image with CISA's ICSNPP parsers, and netbox-ot's Zeek importer turned the logs into these
observation files with the zone map in `.github/bench/zones/ctf-dfrws-2023.csv`. They hold only aggregated
requests per address pair, protocol and function, with counts and first/last seen. No packets, payloads or other
challenge material (memory dumps, CCTV footage, documents) are included.

The zone names, device names and device roles that `manage.py ot_demo --capture` gives these addresses are
netbox-ot's, not the challenge's. They are a reading of the traffic, not facts about the scenario.

The files are regenerated by the Malcolm bench (`.github/bench/README.md`): its `bench-results` artifact holds
`ctf-dfrws-2023.observations/`.
